Space is the Place, 1974, Sun Ra
Space Is the Place is an Afrofuturist science fiction film made in 1972 and released in 1974. It was directed by John Coney, written by Sun Ra and Joshua Smith, and features Sun Ra and his Arkestra. A soundtrack was released on Evidence Records.

The planet it produced
The movie was set in what was the current present time, the early 1970s in the United States of America. The movie really highlighted the oppression and racial prejudice that was happening in the US towards African American showing how unhappy and hopeless they felt living under such system. The movie was quite literal in depicting the racial disparities African Americans were facing by using dialogues and scenes that visually and verbally described how the oppression made African Americans feel invisible and how they felt they were not treated as a real human.

The moment
The premise of the movie revolves around Sun Ra and his jazz group cosmic origins. The plot somewhat resembles an experience Sun Ra claimed to have had one day while he was in a deep religious concentration. In said experience, Sun Ra was transported to a planet he identified as Saturn and was told by the creature to stop attending college as the world is going into a complete chaos and with his music the world would listen.

In a 2008 Black Music Research Journal article, Daniel Kreiss suggested that the movie is a response to the Black Panther Party. The Black Panther Party is known to monitor police activity for any excessive force and misconduct done towards African Americans using people with guns visibly seen.
